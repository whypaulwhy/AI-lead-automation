What you get from an inspection

An inspection takes about 30 minutes. Marco checks every slope, the flashing and vents, and the attic if he can get to it. Afterwards you get his notes, every photo he took, and a written estimate. This is what the notes look like, from a typical call after a hailstorm.

Inspection notes (sample)

House
One-story ranch in Allandale, built in 1964
Roof
Asphalt shingle, about 17 years old
Why we came out
Brown stain on the hallway ceiling after the April hail
Photos
26, all sent with the estimate

  • Plumbing vent above the hallway

    Found: The rubber boot around the pipe has split, and the decking under it is soft. This is where the stain comes from.

    What we'd do: Replace the boot and one sheet of decking. A small repair, about two hours.

  • North and west slopes

    Found: Hail bruises on about 30 shingles, with granules washed into the gutters.

    What we'd do: Mark and photograph each hit for your adjuster. If the claim is approved, replace both slopes.

  • Ridge cap

    Found: Two cap shingles lifted at the east end.

    What we'd do: Re-nail and seal them during the vent repair.

  • Attic

    Found: Dry apart from the spot under the vent. Ventilation looks fine.

    What we'd do: Nothing needed.

Questions

Is the inspection really free?

Yes. You get the notes, photos and a written estimate either way, and you don't owe us anything if you decide not to go ahead.

Do you work with insurance?

Yes. We can meet your adjuster on site and give you photos and notes for your claim.

What if water is coming in right now?

Call (512) 555-0147. We can usually get a tarp on it the same day.

How long does a new roof take?

Most houses take one or two days once the materials are delivered. We tell you the start date and how many days to expect before we begin.

Do you clean up after the job?

Yes. We cover plants and the AC unit before we start, haul away the old roof, and run a magnet over the yard and driveway for nails before we leave.

Do you do gutters or commercial buildings?

Gutters only as part of a roof job, and homes only. For either of those on their own, a specialist will serve you better.
